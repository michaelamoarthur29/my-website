Overview

The Problem: Checkout Friction for Pro Customers

Pro Xtra is Home Depot's loyalty program for contractors and trade professionals. The program depends on Pros being correctly identified and credited at checkout — but that identification step regularly breaks down at the register, creating lost discounts, disputed receipts, and frustrated repeat customers.

To ground the problem, I built out a contractor persona — Marcus T., nicknamed "Stacky" — representing a high-frequency Pro whose checkout experience surfaced the core friction points the rest of the project was built around. The research behind this persona was informed by a structured, paraphrased interview with a Pro Xtra associate, since primary access was limited to that single source — every deliverable I produced was grounded in that interview plus publicly available Home Depot / Pro Xtra materials, with nothing invented or inferred beyond what those sources supported.

Marcus doesn't have time to argue about a discount at the register — if Pro Xtra can't recognize him instantly, he'll take the job's spend somewhere that can.

Process

Two Solutions, Two Philosophies

Rather than pitch a single fix, I scoped and built two full, competing PRDs — one deliberately non-agentic and rules-based, the other agentic — to give the Pro Xtra team a real choice between a safer, incremental solution and a more ambitious, AI-driven one.

01

Smart Pro Identification — Non-Agentic

A rules-based checkout safety net combining Bluetooth/geofencing detection with a physical Pro Xtra card, so a Pro is flagged and recognized before they ever reach the register. Deterministic, low-risk, and fast to implement — designed as the conservative option for a team that needs reliability over ambition.

02

Pro Xtra Recovery Agent — Agentic

A single orchestrator agent coordinating a six-tool pipeline — including receipt parsing, transaction matching, account verification, policy lookup, and fraud-risk detection — to recover missed Pro Xtra credit after the fact. Built with four concrete refusal thresholds, so the agent escalates to a human rather than resolving edge cases on its own.
